Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "Label-Level Hierarchical Selective Acceptance of LLM
Annotations with Certified Error Control" for consideration as a research
article in Data & Knowledge Engineering.

Large language models are now a common first-pass annotator for knowledge bases
and data catalogues, and the labels they produce are frequently hierarchical.
The paper addresses a data-quality question that arises when such labels are
loaded into a database: which machine labels may be written without human
review, with a guaranteed bound on their error rate and as little review effort
as possible. Existing selective-annotation methods decide per item and accept or
reject all labels of an item together. We show that deciding per label, while
keeping the accepted labels ancestor-closed in the taxonomy, accepts
substantially more correct labels at the same certified error level when the
target error is moderate.

The contributions are (i) a formulation of label-level hierarchical selective
acceptance with an explicit reviewing-cost model; (ii) a method that certifies
per-depth thresholds with a learn-then-test procedure in which every
data-dependent design choice is made on data disjoint from the calibration
sample; (iii) a proof that the deployed rule bounds the label-level error ratio with high probability under i.i.d. items, without assuming
independence between the labels of an item; and (iv) an evaluation on four
public hierarchical corpora with two LLM annotators, six baselines, ablations
under fixed test statistics and sensitivity analyses, including the settings in which the method does not win.

We believe the work fits the journal's scope on the construction and
maintenance of data and knowledge bases, in particular data acquisition methods
and integrity of the stored knowledge. All data are public, and the code, the
cached LLM responses and the per-split results are released so that every
number can be reproduced without API access.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration
elsewhere. A related but distinct manuscript by the same first author,
"Calibrating Multi-Axis Multi-Label Annotators with Cross-Axis Co-Occurrence",
is under review through ACL Rolling Review; it studies post-hoc calibration of
confidence scores, whereas the present paper studies certified acceptance
decisions, and the two share no method, experiment or text. Both authors
approved the submission. Hongsheng Wang is also affiliated with Loctek
Ergonomic Technology Corp., as disclosed in the competing-interest statement.

Thank you for considering our work.
